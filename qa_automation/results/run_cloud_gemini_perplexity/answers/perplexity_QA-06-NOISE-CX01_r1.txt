PROMPT:
what are the three SNMPv3 security lveels, what does each one protect, and which one should a company use for production moniotring

ANSWER:
The three SNMPv3 security levels are noAuthNoPriv, authNoPriv, and authPriv. For production monitoring, a company should use authPriv because it provides both authenticated messages and encrypted monitoring data.

What each level protects
Here, “auth” means authentication and message-integrity protection; “priv” means privacy through encryption.

Security level What it protects What it does not protect
noAuthNoPriv Neither message authenticity nor confidentiality. A username alone is not cryptographic proof of identity. No cryptographic protection against forged or modified messages, and no protection against someone reading the monitoring data in transit.

authNoPriv Verifies the message’s origin and detects modification in transit, protecting authenticity and integrity. Does not encrypt the payload, so someone capturing the traffic can still read the monitoring data.

authPriv Provides the authentication and integrity protections above, plus encryption of the SNMP payload to protect confidentiality. Does not replace access controls that determine which devices a user may query or which information they may read or change.

What to use in production
Use authPriv as the production standard—even on an internal management network. Authentication alone prevents neither observation nor disclosure of readable monitoring data; encryption adds that missing protection. Cisco explicitly recommends authPriv for secure deployments and advises avoiding the other two levels for secure production use.
